# Solomon Grinds — design, research and verification

Business information checked: **11 October 2026**. Visual direction rebuilt: **12 October 2026**. This is an **unofficial concept**, created for review and a competitive pitch. It has noindex metadata and a crawler block. No change was made to the business's live website.

## Business and buyer

Solomon Grinds provides small-group Leaving Cert and Junior Cycle tuition in Lucan, serving West Dublin and North Kildare. The current homepage advertises hybrid learning for students unable to attend in person. The venue is Scoil Áine Naofa, Newcastle Road. Booking is through Caitriona Hayes by phone, email or WhatsApp. [Homepage](https://solomongrinds.ie/), [location](https://solomongrinds.ie/location/), [contact](https://solomongrinds.ie/contact-us/).

The 2026/27 dedicated fee page publishes **€20 per class** and **€55 for three subjects per week**. The weekly timetable comprises **23 Leaving Cert sessions and 17 Junior Cycle sessions**, on Wednesdays and Saturdays. Availability is not published and is not inferred. [Fees](https://solomongrinds.ie/fees/), [Leaving Cert](https://solomongrinds.ie/leaving-cert-grinds/), [Junior Cycle](https://solomongrinds.ie/junior-cert-grinds/).

The dedicated Easter page publishes **30 March–3 April 2027**, time blocks and multi-subject fees. Oral preparation lists four languages, a 14:30–17:30 time and a €55 course fee, but no dates. [Easter](https://solomongrinds.ie/easter-revision-leaving-junior-cert/), [orals](https://solomongrinds.ie/oral-preparation/).

**Buyer model — inference:** the likely payer is a parent or guardian, with the student influencing subject and attendance choices. Parent reviews on the site support this interpretation, but it was not tested with customers. The landing concerns are subject/level fit, teaching attention, affordability, timetable compatibility, travel and the student's confidence. Adult students can also enquire directly.

**Moment of decision:** “They teach the right subject and level, at a time we can manage, for a cost I understand; a real person can confirm whether this is suitable.” Competence signals are precise timetables, dated fees, a real local venue and contact, attributed teaching claims and clear uncertainty. Wrong signals would be invented results, fake classroom photos, aggressive grade guarantees or an anonymous booking funnel with fictional availability.

**Independent checks:** the [owner's public professional profile](https://ie.linkedin.com/in/caitriona-hayes-9602426) identifies Caitriona as owner and Maths teacher and reports trading since 2002; only search-visible profile content was available, so it remains an owner-authored public claim. [St Patrick's Parish](https://www.stpatrickslucan.ie/school/scoil-aine-naofa/) independently lists the school. Neither source independently validates every teacher credential, the business's results or current seat availability.

## Awwwards references and revised signature

The live Awwwards education listing and two specific references were inspected:

- [Krackerz Learning on Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/krackerz-learning) and [its live website](https://krackerz.com/): oversized 80px display type at the observed desktop viewport, layered subject-related cards, shaped sections and 12px action radii. It informed typographic confidence and tactile card composition. Its photography, mascot, font and copy were not copied.
- [Superlist on Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/superlist): a strong red field and layered product imagery. It informed the prominence of a product-specific visual scene. Solomon's class cards and scheduling story are original to this business.

The initial repeated split-hero/rectangular-card pattern has been replaced with a centred, oversized opening, a wide three-subject week board, asymmetric exam-cycle cards, a dark scroll scene, a yellow editorial review panel and a red closing section. No stock or generated photograph is presented as the business.

**The Solomon Week** appears first as an animated three-card example and then as a desktop scroll sequence. French oral Wednesday 17:30, English Wednesday 20:00 and Maths Saturday 12:00 settle into a compatible week costing the published €55. The interactive timetable remains a separate working product, with all 40 real sessions and conflict checks. Higher Maths and Biology at Saturday 12:00–13:00 still produce a genuine conflict.

The scroll scene stays pinned above 960px while its three classes assemble. It is unpinned at 960px and below. The cards and total stay fully opaque during the scroll scene; movement provides the staging. The headline, class entrance, cycle switching and planner feedback use GSAP. A replay control lets a reviewer see the opening build again.

Text on the saturated lavender/yellow surfaces uses the dark ink colour. Supporting grey and red are reserved for the lighter surfaces where their contrast passes. Pair calculations and rendered checks are included in the JSON evidence files.

**Type:** self-hosted Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold for display and headings; Open Sans for body and fields. Desktop hero type reaches 103px, inner-page H1 reaches 85px, section H2 reaches 64px. Mobile hero type is 60–83px; inner-page H1 is 43–48px. Body copy is 15–17px. The opening composition is centred and oversized rather than retaining the initial left-text/right-panel template.

**Grid and spacing:** 1360px maximum wrapper, 48px desktop gutters, 32px tablet gutters and 20–24px mobile gutters; 8px base rhythm. The hero becomes a wide board with three ticket columns on desktop and a stacked board on mobile. Exam-cycle panels use unequal columns. Tables retain wrapping and reserved widths.

**Radii:** fields 12px, class rows 16px, tickets 20–23px, filters 22px, planner panels 26–28px, large surfaces 30–48px; actions and status labels use pill shapes. One exam-cycle panel and the mobile week board have larger asymmetric corners. This directly addresses the user's radius feedback.

**Components:** primary actions use red/white; secondary actions use dark boundaries. Class selection is a circle control with explicit pressed state. Status messages use words plus colour. Focus remains outlined and offset. The desktop pin and mobile summary bar never replace the timetable's actual selection controls.

## Revised motion plan

All authored motion is inside `gsap.matchMedia()` with context-wrapped event animations and listener cleanup. One easing curve: **`power3.out`**. GSAP 3.13.0 and ScrollTrigger remain self-hosted. Scrolling is native; no smooth-scroll package or scroll hijacking is used. CSS/HTML content remains visible before JavaScript runs.

| Scene | Trigger | Start → End | Purpose | Mobile | Reduced motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | Page ready | 70% below / 3° / transparent → resting | Establish reading order in the larger composition | Same, compact scale | Static |
| Opening week | Page ready / exam-cycle choice | Offset and rotated class cards → their published week | Connect subjects to days and cost | Stacked cards | Instant update |
| Week assembly | Native scroll | Offset, tilted cards → compatible ordered slots and weekly total | Show one real combination fitting together | Unpinned reveal | Static, no pin or trigger |
| Exam-cycle and course panels | Enter viewport | 55–70px below → resting | Stage the next choice | Shorter travel | Static |
| Planner | Select/remove class | New entries slide into the plan; price settles from 1.16× → 1× | Connect a selection to the resulting week and fee | Same plus summary bar | Instant update |

The pinned scene's cards and price remain fully opaque. The initial QA pass identified a contrast failure caused by translucent cards paused mid-scroll; opacity staging was removed from that scene and the corrected phases were retested.

## Decisions I made for you

1. **Parents/guardians as primary payers, students as co-deciders.** Based on parent-review content and the secondary-school tuition category; labelled inference.
2. **A conversation-led conversion path.** The existing site explicitly books through Caitriona by phone/email/WhatsApp; no live inventory is available.
3. **An expressive student-focused register.** The user requested stronger originality after reviewing the first design; learning-product references and the business's actual subjects support the new direction.
4. **Keep red as the action accent.** The actual Solomon wordmark is red. No competitor colour consensus justified replacing its recognisable cue.
5. **Propose a simplified wordmark/book symbol.** A design judgement using the educational subject and source identity; approval required before official use.
6. **Retain Open Sans body text and introduce Bricolage display type.** The business's original body-font direction stays readable; a licensed expressive display face responds to the Awwwards typography references.
7. **No team/classroom photography.** Existing imagery's authorship and current-business connection could not be verified. Data and typography carry the composition.
8. **€55 takes precedence over €50.** The dated, dedicated fee source is more specific; a visible correction and owner-confirmation requirement remain.
9. **Use Easter 2027, not the old About promotion.** Dedicated updated course source outranks the stale general page.
10. **Remove passed September events from the current pitch.** Compare the source calendar dates with the research date.
11. **Use an attributed start year instead of an anniversary count.** The public owner profile reports 2002; the homepage's 25-year statement has no consistent derivation.
12. **Do not repeat unmeasured competitor discounts or grade promises.** No adequate independent dataset was available.
13. **Build the signature from actual class constraints.** Forty source-listed sessions, one-hour times, a real collision and the published weekly fee explain a practical choice.
14. **One selected class per distinct subject, maximum three subjects for the estimate.** This bounds calculation to the published offer. Multiple sessions of one language or a larger programme need owner-confirmed fee rules; choosing another time replaces the prior session.
15. **Ask for times where subjects have no weekly slot.** Retain the published offering without inventing a timetable.
16. **Do not manufacture Easter subject dates or oral dates.** The Easter source lists time blocks; the oral source explicitly postpones dates.
17. **Confirm hybrid subject coverage individually.** The homepage announces a platform but gives no subject-by-subject operational detail.
18. **Keep payments on the existing invoice page.** It is published and usable as a link; no authority to clone its financial workflow was supplied.
19. **Provide a local draft instead of reusing Contact Form 7.** There is no authorised new endpoint. Labels and native validation still work, and the form explains what happens.
20. **Minimise student information in the draft.** Ask for adult contact and subject needs; do not require a child's name or sensitive history.
21. **A preview-specific privacy notice with a visible legal correction.** Current DPC guidance supports the correction; the real business's lawful bases, retention and processors are undetermined.
22. **Visible GSAP motion with one bounded desktop pin.** The three-class example holds in view as the schedule assembles. Mobile and reduced-motion users get an unpinned or static version.
23. **Mobile plan summary bar.** Visual review showed the top plan could disappear while selecting lower rows; the bar connects those choices back to their count, fee and conflicts.
24. **No embedded map or analytics.** External directions work without unnecessary page-level tracking or a fictional cookie policy.
25. **Noindex public concept on a separate hostname.** The brief requires a shareable unofficial preview and forbids the business's live domain.
26. **Preserve content routes; document commerce migration.** Original sitemap routes show account/payment functions that need owner-authorised continuity, not dummy replacements.

27. **A centred opening, asymmetric cards and deliberately rounded surfaces.** This responds directly to the request to change the repeated layout and make the radius system visible.
28. **Fully opaque pinned cards.** Paused-motion testing found that faded card text was too faint; position and rotation provide the staging instead.
29. **A replay control and tested motion lifecycle.** These make the GSAP work reviewable while preventing duplicate triggers or leaked spacers across breakpoints.

## What the owner must supply or confirm before launch

- Resolve €50/€55 and confirm all weekly, oral and Easter fees; semester amounts; refunds/cancellations; multi-subject conditions; rules for taking multiple sessions of the same subject.
- Confirm current timetables, class/year/level suitability, actual vacancies, holiday closures, course dates and hybrid access for each subject.
- Verify the reported 2002 founding date, teacher identities/qualifications/examiner claims, safeguarding responsibilities and venue access instructions.
- Approve the identity and copy; provide properly licensed current photography only if wanted; approve reuse of the attributed review excerpt.
- Approve a submission endpoint, controller/processor arrangements, retention and updated privacy/cookie policies. Identify who monitors enquiries and set a response process.
- Approve payment/account continuity, route migration and final domain/hosting access; test a real message and an authorised payment before retiring any existing flow.
- Remove preview labels and noindex only after approval; then configure official titles, canonical URLs, business structured data, sitemap, redirects and indexing.

For this business model, a shared enquiry inbox owned by Caitriona is enough initially. If an actual booking calendar is added, use a maintained provider with live capacity, an owner dashboard, confirmations and cancellation handling. Its current class inventory and account access are required; a static calendar cannot reliably monitor or reserve places.
